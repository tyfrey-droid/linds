👟 Sportswear Industry

  • adidas signs Unrivaled as apparel partner — adidas, whose North America headquarters sits in Portland, signed a multi-year deal to become the official uniform and performance-apparel partner of Unrivaled, the women's 3-on-3 basketball league, replacing Under Armour starting with the 2027 season; the deal also covers licensed merchandise and apparel for the league's youth clinics and community programming. (Sportico, Fox Business)
  • On enters football, signs Mbappé — Swiss brand On announced its entry into football, naming Thierry Henry director of football and unveiling Kylian Mbappé as the face of the push, ending the striker's two-decade relationship with his prior outfitter; On's first boot, built on its robotic LightSpray manufacturing process, is due in 2027. (Bloomberg, CNN Business)
  • H&M profit jumps on tariff refunds — H&M's fiscal third-quarter operating profit rose to SEK 6.04 billion from SEK 4.91 billion a year earlier, aided by one-time U.S. tariff refunds it doesn't expect to repeat; the retailer said September sales growth is tracking flat with the prior quarter at just 1% in local currencies, a signal of continued softness across mainstream apparel that also shapes the athletic-wear competitive backdrop. (Bloomberg, Business of Fashion)
  • Puma names new Europe chief — Puma named Fokko de Rooij, a nearly 21-year Nike veteran most recently serving as its EMEA sales VP, as Managing Director for Europe effective Oct. 1, with Daniel Pustina named MD for the DACH/Benelux/Eastern Europe cluster; both report to CEO Arthur Hoeld, who is also temporarily covering the vacant chief commercial officer role. (WWD, The Retail Bulletin)
  • Global fiber output hits record — Textile Exchange's newly released Materials Market Report found global fiber production rose to a record 139 million metric tons in 2025, with polyester still accounting for 59% of output and textile-to-textile recycling still under 1% of total production — a supply-chain data point for apparel sourcing teams. (Sourcing Journal)

🤝 Community Impact & CSR

  • Verizon commits $70M to AI skills training — Verizon launched "AI Skills for America," layering $50 million in new funding onto an existing $20 million reskilling fund to offer free AI training built with IBM, Google, Microsoft, Anthropic, Coursera and OpenAI, delivered locally through workforce nonprofits including Goodwill Industries. (Verizon — company announcement, PYMNTS)
  • Comcast NBCUniversal grants $2.5M to nonprofits — Comcast NBCUniversal's Local Impact Grants program distributed $2.5 million in unrestricted funding to 80 nonprofits across 11 U.S. media markets in its ninth annual round, bringing the program's total since 2018 to $24.3 million across 695 organizations. (NewscastStudio, Comcast — company announcement)
  • Gates Foundation launches AI-language coalition — The Gates Foundation convened a 60-organization coalition, including Anthropic, Google and OpenAI Foundation, around a five-year goal of helping roughly 3.4 billion people who speak underrepresented languages use AI tools in their own language and voice. (U.S. News, Gates Foundation — company announcement)

🌲 Portland

  • Council approves Zenith Energy sale — Portland City Council split 6-6 on transferring Zenith Energy's franchise agreement to Miami-based investment firm I Squared Capital, clearing the way for the sale of its Northwest Portland oil-by-rail terminal; Mayor Keith Wilson cast the tiebreaking vote — his second ever — over objections from the council's progressive wing. (Willamette Week, KPTV)
  • Complaints target police chief over tweets row — At least four Oregon voters filed complaints with the Secretary of State's Elections Division alleging Police Chief Bob Day violated the state law barring public employees from political campaigning, after he publicly said he'd lost confidence in Councilor Mitch Green's oversight of the police bureau following Green's resurfaced 2020-21 anti-police tweets; Green faces reelection in November. (KPTV, OPB)
  • School support-staff union files labor complaint — The Portland Federation of School Professionals, representing about 1,100 classified Portland Public Schools employees, filed unfair-labor-practice charges with the state Employment Relations Board, accusing district leadership of retaliation and union-busting, including firing organizing workers and withholding records needed for union business. (KPTV)
  • City's largest shelter closes after a year — The 200-bed Northrup Street overnight shelter in the Pearl District, run by the Salvation Army, closed for good after housing more than 2,700 people since opening in 2025; Mayor Wilson had flagged the closure as a budget-balancing move, citing it as the city's highest-cost shelter amid declining nightly occupancy. (OPB, KPTV)
