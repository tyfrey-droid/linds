👟 Sportswear Industry

  • Columbia hands Europe hub to GXO — Columbia Sportswear, headquartered in Portland, launched a 10-year strategic logistics partnership with GXO Logistics, which now operates the company's primary continental-Europe distribution hub in Cambrai, France. The deal is part of a broader overhaul of Columbia's European supply chain, following a separate five-year distribution agreement with Maersk for the UK and Ireland signed in August. (GlobeNewswire, SGI Europe)
  • HOKA debuts inside Academy Sports — Academy Sports + Outdoors announced it will begin carrying Deckers' HOKA running shoes in 15 stores across Arkansas, Georgia, Louisiana and Texas and on Academy.com starting in early October. Analysts see the addition as a potential $100 million-plus category for Academy by 2030. (PR Newswire, WWD)
  • Skechers' longtime CFO moves to Levi's — Levi Strauss & Co. named John Vandemore — Skechers' CFO for nine years, a stretch in which Skechers' revenue nearly tripled past $9 billion — as its new finance chief effective Nov. 1, succeeding the retiring Harmit Singh. (Retail Dive, WWD)
  • Birkenstock reworks the Boston clog — Birkenstock partnered with filmmaker and Lyrical Lemonade founder Cole Bennett on a furry, off-white wool version of its 50-year-old Boston clog, unveiled at ComplexCon with a custom buckle dedicated to Bennett's dog; the shoe released Oct. 1 through Birkenstock, Bennett's own site and select retailers. (WWD)

🤝 Community Impact & CSR

  • Walmart commits $15M to food rescue — Walmart granted $15 million to Feeding America to strengthen how food is rescued, transported and delivered across the charitable food system, an investment projected to help the network rescue 500 million pounds of food over the next 18 months. (Walmart — company announcement, Axios)
  • Visa Foundation funds small-business support groups — Visa Foundation committed $2 million — its first U.S. request for proposals — to strengthen nonprofit organizations that support small businesses, planning grants of $100,000 to $250,000 to as many as 12 groups; applications are due Oct. 21. (PYMNTS, Visa — company announcement)
  • Morningstar pledges $5M to Chicago nonprofits — Alongside announcing its new global headquarters at Chicago's Thompson Center, Morningstar committed $5 million over five years to Chicago-area nonprofits, with $4 million administered by United Way of Metro Chicago for multi-year grants focused on financial security and career advancement. (Morningstar — company announcement, GuruFocus)
  • PSEG Foundation backs 80 hunger nonprofits — PSEG Foundation awarded $500,000 to 80 nonprofits fighting food insecurity across New Jersey and Long Island for National Hunger Action Month, the first installment of more than $1 million it plans to distribute this year; the foundation has given over $9.8 million to more than 825 organizations since 2014. (ROI-NJ, PR Newswire — company-sourced wire release)
  • Apparel brands back Ghana waste coalition — Adidas, H&M Group, Inditex and Bestseller joined Ghana's The Or Foundation to launch the Apparel Recovery Coalition at Accra's Kantamanto Market, one of the world's largest secondhand-clothing hubs, committing voluntary funding over two years toward a producer-responsibility blueprint for managing the textile exports that flood West African and Asian markets. (Business of Fashion, Ecotextile News)

🌲 Portland

  • Chamber sues city over Arts Tax hike — The Portland Metro Chamber and a small-business owner sued the city in Oregon Tax Court, arguing the City Council acted unconstitutionally when it voted 7-5 in May to raise the voter-approved Arts Tax from $35 to $50 with future inflation increases, without sending the change back to voters. A win would revert the tax to $35 unless Council refers the increase to the ballot. (Willamette Week, OPB)
  • County to ease East County ambulance times — Multnomah County commissioners advanced a new Ambulance Service Plan that would move Troutdale, Fairview and Wood Village from an 8-minute "urban" emergency-response standard to a 15-minute "suburban" one, with county EMS staff calling the current standard unachievable and citing roughly $6.9 million in annual savings; a final vote is set for Oct. 8. (KGW)
  • Blazers affirm commitment amid arena talks — At the team's preseason media day, Trail Blazers business president Dewayne Hankins said the franchise remains committed to Portland as talks continue over a roughly $586 million publicly funded Moda Center renovation and lease, split among the state, city and county; a council vote is targeted for December alongside a state funding deadline. (The Columbian, KATU)
  • Ex-DA Schmidt accused of retaliation — Kirsten Snowden, chief deputy district attorney at the Multnomah County DA's office, filed suit seeking at least $350,000, alleging former DA Mike Schmidt retaliated against her after she filed a workplace complaint — including by giving her a smaller raise after she backed his 2024 election opponent. The suit cites findings from outside investigators and the state labor bureau. (KPTV)
