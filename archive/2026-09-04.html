👟 Sportswear Industry

  • Lululemon posts steep sales drop, awaits new CEO — Lululemon's fiscal second-quarter results, reported Thursday, showed comparable sales down 9% (Americas down 12%) and net revenue down 4% to $2.4 billion; the company cut its full-year outlook to $10.35–$10.5 billion, from $11–$11.15 billion previously, as former Nike executive Heidi O'Neill prepares to become CEO on Sept. 8. 🌲 Portland

  • Portland to cover transit passes for city staff — Portland City Council voted unanimously Wednesday to fund a one-year pilot providing fully subsidized TriMet, MAX, WES and C-TRAN passes to the city's roughly 9,000 employees, an emergency measure timed to the five-week closure of I-5 southbound at the Rose Quarter starting Sept. 11. (KOIN, Willamette Week)
  • Judge rejects recount in police ballot fight — A federal judge denied a request to recount signatures on the failed "Safer Portland" initiative, which had sought to redirect Portland Clean Energy Fund revenue to police hiring before elections officials ruled it about 6,000 signatures short; the Thursday ruling found the city and county acted in good faith, closing out the petitioners' last legal challenge. (Portland Mercury)
  • Intel contract loss triggers Hillsboro layoffs — Amentum, which provides maintenance and engineering services at Intel's Hillsboro campus, notified the city it is laying off 74 workers after Intel awarded a contract the company held to a competitor; the cuts take effect when the contract expires Oct. 19. (Yahoo Finance)
