🌲 Portland

  • Council approves Moda Center term sheet — Portland City Council voted 8-4 Wednesday to formally adopt a non-binding term sheet for Moda Center renovations — up to $120 million upfront plus up to $275 million in city upkeep through 2047 — as its opening position for a 20-year Trail Blazers lease, adding an amendment charging the team $3.17 million in annual rent. City and team negotiators are set to begin formal lease talks Aug. 17, building toward binding votes from both the city and county in December. (OPB, Portland Tribune)
  • City demands halt to hazmat rail traffic — Days after cadaver dogs indicated a "high likelihood" of human remains in the fire-collapsed Centennial Mills warehouse, city officials learned Union Pacific ran 13 rail cars carrying residual chlorine past the unstable structure on Aug. 11, breaking a safety commitment; City Administrator Raymond C. Lee III formally demanded the railroad halt hazardous shipments through the zone, but freight operations had resumed near the site by Aug. 13 despite the warnings. (KGW, Axios Portland)
  • Five-week I-5 shutdown confirmed — ODOT confirmed southbound I-5 between I-405 and I-84 will fully close for about five weeks starting Sept. 11 for repaving and joint repairs, affecting roughly 120,000 daily vehicles including 11,700 trucks; officials are urging Fremont Bridge detours and warning commutes could take two to three times longer. Matching northbound work is planned for 2027. (Portland Tribune, KATU)
  • Council committee advances PSU theater plan — A Portland City Council committee voted 4-1 to advance Mayor Keith Wilson's plan for a city-PSU partnership to build a roughly 3,000-seat Broadway-capable venue near Portland State, with Keller Auditorium continuing Broadway shows through at least 2030; Councilor Dan Ryan cast the lone no vote, citing insufficient protections against Keller eventually going dark. (KGW, Oregon ArtsWatch)
  • Providence drops Medicare Advantage too — Providence Health Plan said talks to hand off its Medicare Advantage business, covering about 60,000 members, to a national carrier had collapsed, so that coverage line will also shut down at year's end alongside the insurer's already-announced exit from most other lines; roughly 440,000 members in total must find new coverage for 2027. (Willamette Week, KATU)
