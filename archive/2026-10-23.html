👟 Sportswear Industry

  • adidas posts record Q3, lifts outlook — adidas, whose North America headquarters sits in Portland, reported preliminary third-quarter results Tuesday showing revenue up 9% to a record €7.25 billion and operating profit up 37% to €1.008 billion — the first quarter above €1 billion in company history — helped by a roughly €200 million US tariff refund that lifted gross margin to 54.3%. CEO Bjørn Gulden said core sports categories are driving the recovery even as lifestyle footwear still faces discounting pressure, and adidas raised its full-year operating-profit outlook to around €2.5 billion from €2.3 billion; shares rose about 3.3% in Frankfurt trading. (FashionUnited, KSL)
  • Deckers beats EPS, cuts outlook — Deckers Brands, parent of HOKA and UGG, reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of $1.48 billion, short of Wall Street's roughly $1.51 billion estimate, even as adjusted EPS of $2.03 beat expectations; HOKA revenue rose 7% and UGG rose 4%. Management cut full-year revenue guidance to $5.70–5.73 billion from $5.86–5.91 billion, citing heavy US promotional pressure and a European logistics transition, and shares fell more than 7% in after-hours trading. (WWD)

🤝 Community Impact & CSR

  • Anthropic pledges 5% of equity — Anthropic announced the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, pledging 5% of its shares — with up to 2% more at the board's discretion as the company's valuation grows — to a new LLC built to fund traditional nonprofits and support for populations affected by AI-driven labor disruption. Combined with earlier founder and employee pledges, the company says roughly 20% of its equity is now earmarked for public benefit; the fund's governance and first grants haven't been detailed yet. (Al Jazeera, AJC)
  • Tallgrass's $105M gift to Yellowstone — Colorado energy-infrastructure company Tallgrass and nonprofit partner Yellowstone Forever created a $105 million Conservation and Stewardship Fund for Yellowstone National Park, which the National Park Service calls the largest single private gift in its history. The fund will back research, a new visitor-education center at Norris Geyser Basin and a corpus of grants for Wyoming students and families; the Park Service keeps full authority over how funded projects are run. (National Park Service, KUNC)
  • Cyber-defense fund for small utilities — Accenture, industrial-cybersecurity firm Dragos and the SANS Institute launched the Community Cyber Resilience Foundation, committing roughly $10 million in funding and services to give free security tools, hardware and training to under-resourced US water, electric and gas utilities with under $100 million in annual revenue — a group the partners say covers more than 50,000 organizations. The first phase will support up to 500 utilities. (SecurityWeek, Accenture — company announcement)

🌲 Portland

  • 800 city workers could strike — The City of Portland Professional Workers Union voted 93% to authorize a strike after ten months of stalled wage talks, with members able to walk out as soon as Nov. 1; a second city union could vote next week to join, nearly doubling the number of workers out by December. The city says its offer includes about $9.1 million in new spending against the union's roughly $19.8 million ask. (OPB)
  • Superintendent leans toward smaller list — Portland Public Schools Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong said on OPB's Think Out Loud that the district is "leaning into" the 11-school closure scenario rather than the 14-school option, arguing it still leaves roughly 70% of students in sustainably sized schools, and rejected calls to slow the process. Students and parents staged a sit-out at Sunnyside Environmental School, which isn't on the closure list but would lose its K-8 structure and environmental focus under the plan; the board votes in December. (OPB, KPTV)
  • Blazers haven't countered on Moda Center — As the Trail Blazers opened their season with a blowout win over Phoenix, OPB reported the team still hasn't sent the city a written counteroffer to the term sheet councilors approved in August, with rent, arena-worker unionization and a "first-class" standard clause still unresolved. Council President Jamie Dunphy said he won't bring a lease to a vote without those details, even as the city's self-imposed Dec. 31 deadline approaches. (OPB)
