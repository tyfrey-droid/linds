👟 Sportswear Industry

  • Under Armour cuts sales outlook — Under Armour's fiscal first-quarter results (quarter ended June 30) showed revenue down 3% to $1.10 billion, with North America sales down 9% to $610 million; the company now expects full-year revenue to fall at a mid-single-digit rate, worse than its prior guidance, citing softening demand in North America, Asia-Pacific and EMEA. CEO Kevin Plank cited a "challenging consumer demand environment," while profitability guidance held steady. (PR Newswire, Bloomberg)
  • UBS downgrades adidas to Neutral — UBS cut adidas from Buy to Neutral and lowered its price target to €173 from €219, warning that rising oil-linked production costs, a stronger dollar and a growing reliance on lower-margin wholesale sales could squeeze gross margin through 2027–28; shares fell about 3% on the note. Analysts said they remain constructive on adidas' longer-term positioning under CEO Bjørn Gulden. (Investing.com, MarketScreener)
  • JD Sports triples Meadowhall flagship — JD Sports opened a newly expanded flagship at Sheffield's Meadowhall shopping centre, combining five units into a 29,225-square-foot store — about three times its former footprint — which the retailer called a major investment signaling confidence in physical retail. (Retail Gazette, TheIndustry.fashion)
  • Birkenstock board member resigns — Alexandre Arnault, son of LVMH chairman Bernard Arnault, resigned from Birkenstock's board effective August 6 after roughly five years, citing professional commitments — the same reason he gave leaving Moncler's board in July. Birkenstock is searching for an independent director to fill the seat. (FashionUnited, EQS News)

🤝 Community Impact & CSR

  • DICK'S Foundation grants Little League $25K — The DICK'S Sporting Goods Foundation surprised Twin Rivers Little League in New Bern, N.C. with a $25,000 Sports Matter grant during the Little League Softball World Series, earmarked for facility upgrades and removing barriers to youth participation. (Little League — company announcement)
  • Duckhorn Portfolio awards $340K in grants — Wine company The Duckhorn Portfolio named six recipient organizations for its 2026 Founders Fund, awarding a combined $340,000 to support educational programs for historically disadvantaged groups in the communities where its employees live and work; the fund has now given more than $1 million to 16 organizations since launching in 2020. (BevNET, Wine Industry Advisor — company announcement)

🌲 Portland

  • Moda Center funding advances at both levels — Multnomah County commissioners voted 4-1 Thursday to set aside up to $101.6 million for Moda Center renovations, a day after Portland City Council voted 7-5 to advance a non-binding term sheet — $120 million upfront plus up to $275 million in upkeep through 2047 — following three hours of public testimony and the rejection of all 18 proposed amendments. A full council vote is set for August 12; the county's final vote is expected in December. (OPB, Willamette Week)
  • Police-hiring ballot measure disqualified — An initiative to redirect 25% of Portland Clean Energy Fund tax revenue toward hiring about 400 additional police officers officially failed to qualify for the November ballot, falling 6,307 signatures short after more than 70% of rejected signatures came from unregistered or non-Portland voters. Portland Police Association president Aaron Schmautz said backers plan to sue over the decision. (Willamette Week, OPB)
  • Portland tops world's worst air quality — Wildfire smoke from Oregon's record 2-million-plus-acre fire season, including the Grasshopper Fire near Mount Hood, pushed Portland's air quality to "unhealthy" levels; IQAir briefly ranked it the most polluted major city in the world among roughly 120 cities tracked, with advisories overlapping a stretch of hot weather. (OPB, IQAir)
  • Emerald ash borer reaches new Oregon sites — State forestry officials confirmed the invasive emerald ash borer beetle in new northwest Oregon locations, including Salem, Beaverton, Bethany Lake Park and the Oregon Garden in Silverton, along with its first confirmed detection inside an Oregon state park, on the Banks-Vernonia State Trail. (OPB, KPTV)
