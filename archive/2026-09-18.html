👟 Sportswear Industry

(WWD, FashionUnited)
  • Lycra Company taps new CEO — The Lycra Company, a key spandex/elastane supplier to activewear brands, named Hua Du as chief executive effective Sept. 14, succeeding interim CEO Dean Williams as the fiber maker moves past its recent financial restructuring. (SGB Media, Ecotextile News)
  • Garment safety accord hits record scale — A record 300 global apparel and footwear brands and retailers have now signed the International Accord for Health and Safety in the Textile and Garment Industry, covering roughly $26 billion in sourcing across more than 2,000 factories and 3.4 million workers in Bangladesh and Pakistan. (Sourcing Journal, Just Style)

🤝 Community Impact & CSR

  • Duffield Foundation commits $250M to pet care — The Dave & Cheryl Duffield Foundation announced a five-year, $250 million "Big Bet for Pets" initiative to reduce financial barriers to veterinary care for struggling families, opening with $2.5 million in initial unrestricted grants to nonprofit veterinary clinics nationwide — one of the largest private philanthropic commitments ever made to expand access to nonprofit veterinary care. (Chronicle of Philanthropy, PR Newswire)
  • Comcast reports $440M in community giving — Comcast's newly released 2026 Impact Report says the company made $440 million in cash and in-kind community investments in 2025 and has now put roughly $200 billion toward broadband access, jobs and community programs over the past five years, anchored by its $1 billion Project UP digital-equity commitment. (CSRwire — company announcement)
  • Funding gap hits women-of-color nonprofits — New Candid/Philanthropy News Digest research finds nonprofits led by women of color receive grants for day-to-day programming but are largely shut out of the flexible, multi-year general-operating support that helps organizations and their leaders build lasting capacity — a data point relevant to funders rethinking equity in grantmaking strategy. (Philanthropy News Digest)

🌲 Portland

  • Knights pledge $1.1B to Providence — Phil and Penny Knight pledged $1.1 billion to Providence St. Vincent Medical Center, funding Oregon's first dedicated women's hospital and an expansion of cardiac care over a 10-year payout — one of the largest healthcare philanthropic gifts in U.S. history, and following the Knights' $2 billion pledge to OHSU's Knight Cancer Institute last year. (OPB, Portland Tribune)
  • Police union demands Councilor Green resign — Portland Police Chief Bob Day said he no longer has confidence in City Councilor Mitch Green's ability to fairly oversee the police bureau after resurfaced 2020-21 tweets in which Green called officers "monsters" and voiced "pure hatred" for police; the police union has called for his resignation. Green, who faces reelection in November, confirmed the posts were his but said he wouldn't use that language now. (OPB, KGW)
  • Council votes to curb data-center secrecy — All 12 Portland city councilors co-sponsored — a first since the city's charter overhaul — a resolution urging the city administrator not to sign non-disclosure agreements with data center developers and to immediately notify council of inquiries about large power-hungry facilities, a non-binding push for transparency as hyperscale computing projects raise concerns about strain on regional power and water supplies. (KPTV, KATU)
  • PPS bond program flagged for fraud risk — A draft external audit obtained by Willamette Week found Portland Public Schools' $1.8 billion high-school construction bond is at heightened risk of fraud after the district outsourced management to Texas-based contractor Procedeo with insufficient oversight, including unsupported payroll costs and unallowable spending; a former school board member has since filed a prospective recall petition against board chair Eddie Wang over his role in approving the contract. (Willamette Week, Willamette Week)
