👟 Sportswear Industry

  • Columbia posts strong Q3 beat — Columbia Sportswear, headquartered in Portland, reported third-quarter net sales up 2% to $962.6 million and diluted EPS of $1.66, sharply ahead of the $67.4 million operating income (7.1% of sales) posted a year earlier when prAna and Mountain Hardwear impairment charges weighed on results; this quarter's operating income reached $109.6 million (11.4% of sales). The company raised the low end of its full-year EPS guidance to $4.65–$4.90, even as roughly $30 million in wholesale shipments shifted from the third quarter into the fourth. (SEC filing)
  • Crocs raises outlook a third time — Crocs reported third-quarter revenue up 4.6% to $1.04 billion and net income of $216.8 million ($4.52 diluted EPS), against $145.8 million ($2.70) a year earlier, with gross margin boosted in part by IEEPA tariff refunds. HEYDUDE returned to growth with revenue up 6%, and the company raised its full-year enterprise revenue outlook for the third straight quarter while buying back $250 million in stock. (SEC filing)
  • VF Corp beats on North Face, Altra — VF Corporation reported fiscal second-quarter results Wednesday showing revenue of about $2.7 billion (up 1% excluding the divested Dickies brand) and adjusted EPS of $0.70 against a Wall Street consensus near $0.53. The North Face grew 4% to $1.2 billion and Altra rose more than 40%, while Vans remained the weak spot, down 10% to $547.6 million even as its global retail revenue grew for the first time in nearly five years; VF reaffirmed its full-year outlook. (FashionUnited)

🤝 Community Impact & CSR

  • Airbnb commits $500M to housing — Starting Wednesday, Airbnb began directing 10 cents from every booked stay, excluding cancellations, to its nonprofit Airbnb.org, aiming to raise $500 million over five years toward a goal of providing 1 million free nights of emergency and disaster housing a year by year five — up from roughly 100,000 nights this year. The company says the money comes from its own share of booking revenue, with no added cost to guests or hosts. (Skift, PhocusWire)
  • Herbalife renews Red Cross pledge — The American Red Cross announced Tuesday that Herbalife has renewed its roughly 30-year partnership with a $1 million in-kind commitment of protein bars and bites for blood donors under the Blood Saves Lives program, timed to the company's October "Global Month of Purpose" volunteering push. (American Red Cross)

🌲 Portland

  • School closure protests escalate — Families packed a regional meeting at Jefferson High School and rode bikes across the St. Johns Bridge on Oct. 24 to oppose Portland Public Schools' proposed closure of up to 14 schools. OPB reported that Portland and Salem-Keizer are pursuing parallel closure plans that could shutter a combined 20 schools between the two districts, driven by budget gaps of $65 million and $46 million respectively; Portland's board votes in December. (KPTV, OPB)
  • Council bans guns in city buildings — Portland City Council voted 12-0 Wednesday to end the concealed-handgun-license exemption for City Hall, the Portland Building and the 1900 Building, relying on authority granted by last year's Senate Bill 243. The city will post signage at the affected entrances and give notice and a chance to comply before referring violations to law enforcement. (KATU, KPTV)
