👟 Sportswear Industry

  • Nike launches Studio Fleece line — Nike introduced Studio Fleece, a new sportswear system of hoodies, sweatpants and quarter-zips developed with research from more than 300 women, launching on nike.com and select retailers with a campaign fronted by Sha'Carri Richardson, Alysa Liu, Jazzy Davidson and K-pop star Karina. Trade press framed it as part of CEO Elliott Hill's push to reinvigorate Nike's core sportswear business. (Nike News, Retail Dive)
  • Nike becomes Rush Soccer's apparel partner — Nike signed a global partnership with Rush Soccer, the world's largest youth soccer organization with more than 60,000 players across 40-plus countries, covering player development, club growth, coaching education and youth participation programs. Financial terms weren't disclosed. (Rush Soccer, SGB Media)
  • adidas posts record revenue, stock plunges — adidas reported record second-quarter net sales of €6.7 billion, up 14% currency-neutral, driven by World Cup demand — 14 of 48 competing nations wore adidas kits, pushing jersey sales to roughly four times 2022's tournament total. But operating profit rose just 5% to €574 million, missing estimates after the company overspent its World Cup marketing budget by €212 million, and shares fell more than 18% in a single day — the steepest drop in company history since its 1995 IPO. Full-year revenue guidance was raised to 9–10% growth. (adidas Group, WWD)
  • Columbia beats on tariff refund boost — Columbia Sportswear, headquartered in the Portland area, reported second-quarter net sales of $614.4 million, up 2%, with earnings lifted by a roughly $78 million IEEPA tariff refund. The company raised full-year gross-margin guidance to 52.1–52.3% while holding sales guidance, as international growth offset continued U.S. softness; shares fell after-hours on cautious near-term guidance. (Columbia Sportswear)
  • VF Corp tops targets, names new CFO — VF Corporation, parent of Vans, The North Face and Timberland, reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $1.67 billion, ahead of its own guidance, as The North Face grew 6% and Timberland 4% while Vans kept sliding 8–9% globally. VF raised full-year guidance and named COO Abhishek Dalmia to add the CFO title effective August 1, succeeding Paul Vogel. (VF Corporation, WWD)
  • Puma narrows loss in turnaround — Puma reported second-quarter sales of €1.69 billion, down 9.4% currency-adjusted amid its ongoing "reset," but narrowed its net loss to €72.8 million from €247 million a year earlier and lifted gross margin roughly 180 basis points, aided by tariff refunds and lower sourcing costs. Free cash flow more than tripled to €328.8 million, and the company confirmed its full-year outlook. (PUMA, Bloomberg)

🌲 Portland

  • Daimler shuts Swan Island plant — Daimler Truck North America announced it will end Freightliner and Western Star production at its Swan Island facility by late October, cutting about 375 jobs after 84 years of manufacturing there and shifting the work to Carolina plants. The company will keep its North American headquarters and a newly opened $40 million engineering facility on Swan Island, and says it will offer severance and transfer opportunities. (KPTV, KATU)
  • Blazers call Moda Center deal a "non-starter" — In their first public City Council appearance since lease talks intensified, Trail Blazers representatives told council Thursday that the city's draft term sheet for the $600 million Moda Center renovation and 20-year lease was a "non-starter," laying out the team's objections publicly for the first time and fueling relocation speculation as the city and county near their funding decisions. (OPB, KPTV)
  • Keller Auditorium vote delayed — Portland City Council pushed back its vote on Mayor Keith Wilson's two-venue performing-arts plan — a roughly 3,000-seat new Broadway venue at Portland State University plus a downsized, renovated Keller Auditorium — from July 29 to August 11 amid uncertainty over financing, after an afternoon of public testimony. (Willamette Week, KPTV)
  • Police-hiring measure fails signature check — A ballot initiative to redirect 25% of Portland Clean Energy Fund tax revenue toward hiring about 400 additional police officers failed the first level of Multnomah County signature verification, with only about 32,880 of the roughly 40,437 required valid signatures found in the sample; the Safer Portland campaign is disputing the review. (Willamette Week)
  • Ex-board members oppose Brim-Edwards bid — Seven current and former Portland Public Schools board members published a letter arguing Commissioner Julia Brim-Edwards, a candidate for Multnomah County Chair, showed "ineffective" leadership during her PPS tenure; Brim-Edwards said her record "speaks for itself." (Willamette Week)
