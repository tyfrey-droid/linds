🌲 Portland

  • TriMet's biggest cuts in years begin — TriMet confirmed sweeping service changes take effect Sunday, Aug. 23: 34 lines are being adjusted, two low-ridership bus routes (82-South Gresham and 153-Stafford/Salamo) are being eliminated, and the MAX Green Line is being shortened to run only between Gateway and Clackamas Town Center, forcing downtown-bound riders to transfer. The agency says the cuts are needed to close a $158 million long-term budget deficit. (KPTV, KATU)
  • Centennial Mills demolished, Amtrak fully restored — Crews demolished the fire-damaged Centennial Mills warehouse this week, explosively removing its rooftop water tower Monday, Aug. 17, then pulling down most of the remaining structure by Tuesday — far faster than the city's initial weeks-long estimate. Regular Amtrak Cascades service between Portland and Seattle, suspended since the fire, fully resumed Wednesday, Aug. 19. (Portland.gov, KGW)
  • Records show city coordinated messaging for Urban Alchemy — Public records obtained by Portland Mercury show Portland Solutions, the city's shelter-system hub, spent months working with the PR firm for shelter operator Urban Alchemy — sharing draft responses to reporters and flagging story timing — as complaints against the nonprofit, which holds the city's largest homeless-services contract, reached at least 156 since 2023. (Portland Mercury)
  • Ethics panel to probe ex-Home Forward CEO — The Oregon Government Ethics Commission voted 8-0 on Aug. 14 to formally investigate Ivory Mathews, who resigned as CEO of Multnomah County's public housing authority in April amid scrutiny of her travel spending. A complaint alleges more than $100,000 in agency-funded travel over three years, including a Hawaii conference trip, in possible violation of state conflict-of-interest law. (Willamette Week, KOIN)
