👟 Sportswear Industry

  • Dick's slashes outlook on Foot Locker slump — Dick's Sporting Goods' fiscal second-quarter results missed Wall Street estimates ($3.53 adjusted EPS on $5.59 billion revenue, versus expectations of $3.76 and $5.65 billion) and the company cut full-year guidance, citing a 3.6% comparable-sales decline at its Foot Locker banner amid weak conditions across the athletic footwear market. (New Balance)

🤝 Community Impact & CSR

  • BackBlack push targets Black Gen Z donors — The GivingTuesday-style August fundraising campaign BackBlack, which has directed more than $12 million to Black-led nonprofits over three years, is for the first time recruiting content creators to reach Black Gen Z donors rather than relying solely on wealthier institutional giving; last year's campaign drew more than 130,000 donors, most giving under $100. (Fortune, Chronicle of Philanthropy)
  • New data: corporate-giving gains hide a pullback — Candid's analysis of CECP's Giving in Numbers 2026 dataset found median total community investment rose to $23.5 million in 2025, but only because top-quartile givers pulled the average up — 52% of companies in the matched set actually gave less than the year before, a trend Candid ties to a new federal tax rule requiring corporate charitable deductions to clear a 1%-of-pretax-income floor starting this year. (Candid)
  • Co-op sponsors Special Olympics GB games — UK retailer Co-op and its media arm became official sponsors of Special Olympics Great Britain's National Summer Games, a Birmingham event drawing more than 1,200 athletes Aug. 27–30; a related "Meals That Matter" campaign with Coca-Cola Europacific Partners has raised more than £200,000 for the organization since 2025. (Grocery Gazette — company announcement)

(KPTV)
