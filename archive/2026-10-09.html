👟 Sportswear Industry

  • Lululemon's new CEO reshapes leadership — New CEO Heidi O'Neill, a 25-year Nike veteran who took over in September, made her first major organizational move: hiring former Nike and Athleta executive Maggie Gauger as chief product officer and ex-Walmart Canada executive Joseph Godsey as chief operating officer, while longtime executives Ted Dagnese and Nikki Neuburger depart the company in November. (Retail Dive, BNN Bloomberg)
  • Anta completes its Puma takeover — Chinese sportswear giant Anta Sports officially closed its roughly €1.5 billion purchase of a 29.06% stake in Puma from the Pinault family's Artémis SAS, becoming Puma's largest shareholder. Anta said it will seek supervisory-board representation but has ruled out a full takeover, and Puma's CEO called the deal a "vote of confidence" in the brand. (FashionUnited, Retail Dive)

🌲 Portland

  • Council weighs concealed-gun ban at City Hall — Portland City Council is considering an ordinance, sponsored by Council President Jamie Dunphy, that would bar concealed-handgun permit holders from carrying firearms into City Hall, the Portland Building and the 1900 Building, using authority restored by Oregon's 2025 SB 243. The city attorney has warned violators could face misdemeanor charges despite holding a valid permit, and Councilor Eric Zimmerman called the measure a "significant legal risk." (KPTV, KOIN)
  • PPS narrows its school-closure scenarios — Portland Public Schools presented its board two narrowed closure scenarios Monday — one closing 14 schools, another closing 11 — down from an earlier 20-school proposal aimed at closing a roughly $50 million shortfall. Hundreds of students, parents and staff packed Tuesday's board meeting urging a delay, and Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong said the district is unlikely to add schools beyond those already named; a final board vote is expected in December. (OPB, Willamette Week)
  • Council races heat up in Districts 3 and 4 — With the Nov. 3 election approaching, OPB previewed Portland's only two City Council races on this year's ballot: District 3, the city's most left-leaning district, where one challenger has Mayor Keith Wilson's endorsement, and District 4, its most affluent and politically moderate district — both with all incumbents facing a crowded field of challengers under ranked-choice voting. (OPB, OPB)
