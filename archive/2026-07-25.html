👟 Sportswear Industry

  • Deckers beats estimates, raises guidance — Deckers Brands, parent of HOKA and UGG, reported first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $1.02 billion (up 5.7%) and earnings of 94 cents per share, ahead of analyst estimates, and raised its full-year outlook. Investors read the modest size of the guidance raise as a caution sign for the sector — Adidas and Puma shares dipped in European trading the next morning. (Deckers Brands, Investing.com)
  • Tariff regime shifts under apparel — The 10% Section 122 import surcharge expired July 24, but USTR immediately moved to impose new Section 301 tariffs — 10% to 12.5% — on 60 economies tied to forced-labor import enforcement, leaving apparel importers only a brief window before new duties took hold. The uncertainty over rates has been pressuring sourcing decisions across the industry all month. (Fibre2Fashion, Fibre2Fashion)
  • Puma names first e-commerce VP — Puma appointed Dusan Hamlin, a longtime Adidas and Reebok digital executive, to the newly created role of Vice President of E-Commerce, reporting to chief commercial officer Matthias Bäumer. The hire follows Puma's move to split its direct-to-consumer business into separate global retail and e-commerce units. (PUMA, WWD)

🤝 Community Impact & CSR

  • New Balance donates shuttered factory — Voters in Norridgewock, Maine approved accepting the donation of a 7.4-acre former New Balance factory from the company, 180–143. New Balance will also cover an estimated $200,000–$225,000 in annual upkeep for two years while the town lines up redevelopment funding, including federal earmarks. (Central Maine)
  • adidas Foundation opens crisis-recovery partnership search — The adidas Foundation launched a global search for one to three strategic partner organizations to help design and run a new multi-year "Recovery Through Sport" program supporting children and youth affected by crisis and displacement, backed by a total funding commitment of €6 million. (adidas Foundation — foundation announcement)

🌲 Portland

  • Portland Fire pushes Moda Center funding — Portland's WNBA team publicly weighed in for the first time on the Moda Center renovation fight, with its president telling Multnomah County commissioners the county should approve its full $101 million share of the roughly $600 million overhaul. A competing amendment would cut the county's commitment to about $65 million; the team says current locker rooms and training facilities weren't built to support a second pro franchise. (OPB)
  • PSU finalizes layoffs, two closures — Portland State University announced its final retrenchment plan: closing the University Studies and Conflict Resolution departments and cutting 36 full-time positions, as it works to close a $35 million two-year budget shortfall. The faculty union says it will continue pushing back on the cuts. (OPB, Inside Higher Ed)
  • Preschool for All's outlook improves — New Portland State Population Research Center estimates presented to Multnomah County commissioners show the county's universal preschool program will need roughly 3,000 fewer seats than originally planned — about 8,000 versus 11,200 by 2030 — meaningfully brightening the program's long-term financial picture. (Willamette Week)
